Almost everyone lives in or near a city, and some people taking this course will just want to find out more about the forces that shape where they live. Other people who enroll in our course will come from the design professions: architects, landscape architects, urban planners, or students in these subjects.

When we talk about design, it’s not the term about the appearance of the things. It’s actually more important of that, it’s the process of determining the form and character of the city (travel long or short distances). But it’s not like designing a building and making a complete thing, cities are never completed. They are in constant flux and constantly being redesigning.


Governments and utilities decide where to locate water and sewer pipes, electricity, phone and cable wires, highways and bridges, trains and transit, airports and ports – and all these decisions have a powerful shaping effect on cities.

Sometimes people talk about cities as if they are outside people’s control, like the weather. We are using the word designing in the name of our course, because everything that happens to shape cities is actually the result of decisions made by governments, business investors, and citizens.

Users are the sufferers of the mistakes made by planners, so lets give everyone a chance to participate and and learn things from unexpected corners as well.
